# Trigger Log

Every forward-looking claim published in The Market Lens and its companion instruments carries a **pre-committed trigger** — a named future release or dated event that would confirm it or mark it wrong. This file is the complete list, append-only.

**Why this exists.** A framework that only confirms is not a framework. Publishing the falsification criterion *before* the data arrives is the only way a reader can tell whether a call was right or merely restated after the fact. Entries are never edited after grading; corrections are appended below the original.

**How to read a row.** `OPEN` means the test date has not arrived. `HIT` means the stated condition occurred. `MISS` means it did not. `PARTIAL` means the direction was right and the magnitude or timing was not — the same standard used in the forecast log of the companion research repository.

### T-001 · The long-end pain threshold
**Set:** 2026-08-19 · **Source:** The Market Lens, long-bond report
**Claim:** 5.337% on the 30-year Treasury is the level at which fiscal authority intervened. On 2026-08-18 the yield reached that level — its highest since June 2007 — and on 2026-08-19 the Treasury announced it would at least double long-end buybacks.
**Test:** Whether the 30-year holds below 5.337% without further intervention.
**Grades HIT if:** the 30-year stays below 5.337% through 2026-11-04 with no enlargement of the buyback program.
**Grades MISS if:** the 30-year exceeds 5.337% at any weekly close before that date.
**Status:** OPEN. As of 2026-08-28 the 30-year sits near 5.20%, having retraced from the high.

### T-002 · The buyback kill-switch
**Set:** 2026-08-19 · **Source:** The Market Lens, long-bond report
**Claim:** The enlarged buyback program ($2B → at least $4B per operation, 10–30yr sectors) is signalling rather than scale — roughly 0.54% of the $739B quarterly borrowing estimate. Relief lasted one trading day.
**Test date:** **2026-11-04**, the quarterly refunding review. This is both the standard refunding date, set long in advance, and the day after the November 3 midterm elections. Both facts are stated; no inference is drawn from their coincidence.
**Grades toward durable official management if:** buybacks are extended or increased at that review.
**Grades as a two-month reprieve if:** they lapse and the 30-year retests 5.337%.
**Grades the underlying thesis MISS if:** the 30-year falls below 5.00% without further intervention.
**Status:** OPEN.

### T-004 · Global divergence versus convergence
**Set:** 2026-08-09 · **Source:** The World Lens
**Claim:** Major-economy inflation rates are diverging, not converging — a spread of roughly 3 points between the highest (US) and lowest (China), and widening. Country-specific conditions dominate over any shared global shock.
**Grades MISS if:** the spread narrows below **1.5 points** within two quarters, indicating a single global force has reasserted itself.
**Status:** OPEN. **Amendment 2026-09-01:** the finding requires splitting. Inflation rates remain dispersed, but long-term *yields* are converging upward across Japan, Germany, the UK and the US simultaneously. Inflation diverging and term premia converging are compatible and were conflated in the original statement. Graded as stated; the amendment is recorded rather than substituted.

## Graded triggers

### G-001 · Long-end relief will come from the fiscal side, not the Federal Reserve
**Set:** 2026-08-07 · **Graded:** 2026-08-19 · **Result: HIT**
The Market Lens stated across the August 7 and August 14 editions that "anyone waiting for long-term borrowing costs to fall materially is waiting on the fiscal picture, not the employment picture," and that friendly inflation data with an expected policy pause and a long end that would not fall was evidence long rates were being set by deficits rather than by the Fed.
On 2026-08-19 the Treasury — not the Federal Reserve — intervened directly in the long end, and the 30-year fell roughly 9bp on the announcement. Two cooler inflation prints the prior week had moved it almost nothing.
**Recorded caveat:** the direction was right and the durability was not tested by the claim. The relief lasted one trading day, which the original claim did not anticipate either way.

### G-002 · Credit would not follow equities or rates
**Set:** 2026-07-31 · **Graded:** 2026-08-28 · **Result: HIT**
Successive editions stated that stress was confined to rates and had not crossed into credit pricing. The high-yield option-adjusted spread has held near 2.84% through a 19-year high in the 30-year, a 704-point Dow day, a payroll contraction and a consumer-spending decline.
**Caveat:** the spread has not been refreshed to a verified figure since 2026-07-30 and is carried as last-confirmed. The claim is graded on that basis and should be re-verified.

## Corrections and retractions

### C-001 · National inventory turn overstated
**Published:** 2026-08-14 · **Corrected:** 2026-09-01
The August 14 housing section led on Freddie Mac's description of "for-sale inventory improving from the limited supply seen in recent years" and treated it as a confirmed national supply turn. The NAR series does not support that: existing-home inventory *fell* 1.9% in July to 1.54 million and months' supply was unchanged at 4.6. The loosening is real in new construction and was not yet visible in resale. The distinction was blurred and should not have been.

### C-002 · Board rows published stale
**Published:** 2026-08-21 · **Corrected:** 2026-08-28
Two Redline Board rows (10-year and 30-year Treasury) were published carrying August 14 values, and a federal-interest row reported as added was absent. Cause was a scripting error during the weekly update that silently discarded part of the edit set. Both corrected in the August 28 edition. Any reader using the August 21 file should treat those rows as of the prior week.
